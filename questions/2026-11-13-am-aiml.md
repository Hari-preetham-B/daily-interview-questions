# 2026-11-13 AM — AIML (Medium)

## Question
You have a binary classification task where only 1% of the instances belong to the positive class. A model trained on this data reports 99.5% accuracy but only 20% recall. Why can accuracy be misleading in this setting, and what two evaluation metrics would you recommend instead? How can one of those metrics be used to choose a decision threshold for the classifier?

## Hint
Consider metrics that focus on the minority class rather than overall correctness, and think about how a curve of two rates can guide threshold selection.

## Answer
Accuracy can be misleading because it is dominated by the majority class; a model that predicts every instance as negative will still achieve 99% accuracy. Two more informative metrics for highly imbalanced data are the Precision‑Recall curve (specifically its area, AUPRC) and the F1‑score (the harmonic mean of precision and recall). By plotting the Precision‑Recall curve, you can select a threshold that balances precision and recall to meet a business‑driven trade‑off; for example, choose the threshold that maximizes the F1‑score or that achieves a desired minimum precision while maintaining acceptable recall. This approach directly optimizes the model’s performance on the minority class rather than on overall accuracy.
