## Question
What is the reparameterization trick in Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), why is it necessary for training via backpropagation, and how is it implemented mathematically?

## Hint
Consider why direct sampling from a learned probability distribution creates a bottleneck for gradient propagation during backpropagation.

## Answer
In Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), the encoder outputs parameters of a latent probability distribution, typically mean μ and standard deviation σ. Directly sampling a latent vector z ~ N(μ, σ²) introduces a non-differentiable stochastic node into the computational graph, which blocks gradient flow during backpropagation. The reparameterization trick resolves this by expressing z deterministically as z = μ + σ ⊙ ε, where ε is an independent noise variable sampled from a standard normal distribution N(0, I) and ⊙ denotes element-wise multiplication. By isolating the stochasticity into ε, the sampling step becomes differentiable with respect to μ and σ, allowing standard backpropagation and gradient descent to update the encoder parameters.
