## Question
Explain the difference between the Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve and the Precision-Recall (PR) curve. Why is the PR curve typically preferred over the ROC curve when evaluating binary classification models on highly imbalanced datasets?

## Hint
Consider how False Positive Rate (used in ROC) and Precision (used in PR) are calculated, specifically paying attention to the role of True Negatives when negative instances dominate the dataset.

## Answer
The ROC curve plots the True Positive Rate (TPR / Recall) against the False Positive Rate (FPR = FP / (FP + TN)), while the PR curve plots Precision (TP / (TP + FP)) against Recall (TP / (TP + FN)). In highly imbalanced datasets where the negative class heavily outnumbers the positive class, the denominator of FPR (FP + TN) is extremely large due to the high count of True Negatives. Consequently, a large increase in False Positives results in only a tiny, barely noticeable change in FPR, making the ROC curve appear deceptively optimistic. In contrast, Precision does not incorporate True Negatives in its denominator and instead focuses strictly on the total predicted positives. Therefore, the PR curve is far more sensitive to False Positives and provides a much clearer, more realistic representation of model performance on skewed data.
