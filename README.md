### 🌅 Morning (2026-08-25) — AIML

**Compare L1 (Lasso) and L2 (Ridge) regularization. How do their penalty terms differ mathematically, and why does L1 regularization tend to produce sparse weight vectors while L2 does not?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint</summary>

Think about the shape of their constraint regions (contours) in parameter space and where the unconstrained loss contours are likely to intersect them.

</details>

<details>
<summary>✅ Answer</summary>

L1 regularization adds a penalty proportional to the sum of absolute values of the weights ($\\lambda \\sum |w_i|$), while L2 regularization adds a penalty proportional to the sum of squared weights ($\\lambda \\sum w_i^2$). Geometrically, in 2D parameter space, the L1 constraint region forms a diamond shape with sharp corners on the coordinate axes, whereas L2 forms a smooth circle. The loss function contours are most likely to intersect the L1 diamond at one of its axis corners, which drives the corresponding parameter exactly to zero, producing a sparse model useful for feature selection. In contrast, the smooth L2 sphere results in intersections off the axes; it continuously shrinks all weights toward zero proportionally to their size, but rarely forces them to be exactly zero.

</details>
