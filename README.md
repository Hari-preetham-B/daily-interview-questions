## 📅 Today's Question — 2026-08-21 (AIML)

**Explain the vanishing and exploding gradient problems in deep neural networks. What causes them, and what are two common techniques used to mitigate each?**

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint</summary>

Consider how gradients are computed using the chain rule during backpropagation through many layers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>✅ Answer</summary>

The vanishing and exploding gradient problems occur during backpropagation in deep networks when gradients become extremely small or infinitely large as they propagate back through layers. This is caused by the repeated multiplication of weights and activation function derivatives (via the chain rule); values consistently less than 1 cause gradients to vanish, while values greater than 1 cause them to explode. Vanishing gradients prevent early layers from updating their weights, while exploding gradients cause unstable training and model divergence. To mitigate vanishing gradients, one can use non-saturating activation functions like ReLU, proper weight initialization (e.g., He or Xavier initialization), or residual connections. To handle exploding gradients, gradient clipping and weight regularization (L1/L2) are effective techniques.

</details>
