## Question
Explain how Focal Loss modifies standard Cross-Entropy loss to address severe class imbalance and the dominance of easy negative examples in single-stage object detectors. What are the specific mathematical roles of the focusing parameter (gamma) and the alpha-balancing factor (alpha)?

## Hint
Think about how standard cross-entropy treats well-classified (easy) examples versus misclassified (hard) examples, and how multiplying the loss by a term involving the estimated probability dynamic suppresses easy examples.

## Answer
Standard Cross-Entropy (CE) loss assigns equal weight to all samples, allowing a vast majority of easy-to-classify background examples to dominate the cumulative loss and gradients during training. Focal Loss modifies CE by adding a modulating factor (1 - p_t)^gamma, where p_t is the model's estimated probability for the ground truth class. When an example is easily classified (p_t approaches 1), the modulating factor approaches zero, dynamically down-weighting its loss contribution so the model focuses gradient updates on hard, misclassified examples. The focusing parameter gamma (gamma >= 0) smoothly controls the rate at which easy examples are suppressed (higher gamma suppresses easy samples more aggressively). Additionally, the alpha-balancing factor alpha in [0, 1] acts as a static weight to address raw class frequency imbalance between positive and negative classes.
