### 🌅 Morning (2026-11-01) — AIML

**What is the vanishing gradient problem in deep neural networks, why does it occur, and how can it be mitigated? Illustrate with a simple modification to a standard RNN cell that addresses this issue.**

<details>
<summary>💡 Hint</summary>

Consider how gradients are multiplied across many layers or time steps and the role of activation functions.

</details>

<details>
<summary>✅ Answer</summary>

The vanishing gradient problem arises when gradients flowing back through many layers or time steps shrink exponentially, often due to activation functions like sigmoid or tanh whose derivatives are bounded by 1. This causes earlier layers to learn very slowly or not at all. Two common mitigations are (1) using ReLU or its variants (which have larger gradients for positive inputs) and (2) employing gating mechanisms such as in LSTM or GRU cells that provide shortcut connections for gradients. A simple way to modify a vanilla RNN cell is to replace the tanh activation with a ReLU and add a residual connection: 

python
h_t = torch.relu(Wx @ x_t + Wh @ h_{t-1} + b) + h_{t-1}


The residual term helps keep the gradient magnitude stable while ReLU mitigates saturation, thereby reducing the vanishing gradient effect.

</details>
