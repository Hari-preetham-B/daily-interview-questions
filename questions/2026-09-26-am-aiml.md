## Question
Describe the process of transfer learning in deep neural networks. When would you choose to freeze certain layers, and what are the trade‑offs in terms of training speed, generalization, and risk of overfitting?

## Hint
Think about how lower layers capture generic features versus higher layers capturing task‑specific patterns, and consider dataset size.

## Answer
Transfer learning involves taking a model pre‑trained on a large source dataset (e.g., ImageNet) and adapting it to a target task with limited data. The typical approach is to reuse the network’s convolutional backbone, optionally freezing its weights, and retrain only the final classification layers on the new data. Freezing layers is useful when the target dataset is small or similar to the source domain, because it prevents catastrophic forgetting of generic visual features and reduces the number of trainable parameters, which speeds up training and lowers memory usage. However, if the target domain differs substantially, freezing may limit the model’s ability to learn new feature hierarchies, potentially hurting performance. Conversely, unfreezing more layers allows the network to adjust to domain‑specific cues but increases the risk of overfitting to the limited target data and requires longer training times. Thus, the decision to freeze layers balances training efficiency against the need for task‑specific representation learning and generalization.
